On your Mac

Sanduhr für Claude

Your claude.ai usage meters, living on the desktop and around the notch. And one Settings page that reads your whole Claude Code setup and carries it to another Mac.

Native Swift, signed with Developer ID and notarized by Apple. Homebrew or a .dmg. macOS 14 or newer, Apple silicon and Intel. No telemetry.

Your meters live on the desktop.

A fresh install starts with Desk on and your meters already there, drawn under your windows in a handwritten face. The widget becomes the toolbox you open for the deep tools.

Since 2.29.0, Just the Desk on the welcome sheet starts with the Desk and a three-step tour, no Claude subscription needed. Pacing still needs one.

Meters on your desktop

Your session and weekly limits drawn as bars under your windows, each with a pace tick and its reset time, in any of eight places you arrange right on the desktop. A limit that's nearly full with plenty of time left turns red, and you set how full and how far from a reset, per limit. A two-finger click on a meter opens that limit's own menu.

The rest of Desk

The time, today's next meetings with one-click join, and a daily line of your own, in the handwritten EsteFont that ships with the app. Calendar access is asked for only if you turn meetings on, and nothing leaves your Mac. Corners on the Dock's side stay clear of it, and rise with a Dock that hides.

Under your files

Since 2.27.0, Settings → Desk → Clicks has Keep the Desk under desktop icons: the Desk moves below the Finder's icons, so files on the desktop sit on top of its text and can be selected, opened and dragged wherever they are. Meeting rows, watchers, now playing and the account name keep their clicks through a transparent layer; the clock's, message's and meters' menus wait until the option is off. Off by default.

A plate behind every piece

Desk Look's Plate behind the text draws a soft dark plate behind each piece, for a desktop with files under it. On for a new install, untouched for an existing one.

Arrange Desk

Drag each piece where you want it. While arranging, every piece has Hide and a Hidden strip brings one back; Done writes the layout once, Cancel drops it.

Limits that come and go

A temporary limit, like a Weekly Special, shows up when Anthropic adds it and can be hidden from its own meter's menu. Hidden Limits brings it back, and the same menu turns that limit's warnings off and on.

A line on the lock screen

Settings → Desktop → Lock Screen puts one short line under the lock screen clock: your Claude usage, when it resets, and how many watchers are waiting for you, after a prefix of your own if you like. Anyone at the screen can read it, so only counts, percents and times get through; paths, emails, addresses and tokens are dropped, and the page previews the exact line. Turning it on asks for an administrator password once, for a small helper that sets the message. Off by default, and turning it off brings back any message you had there before.

The notch, your way.

Each wing, your pick

On MacBooks with a camera notch, pick what the left wing, the right wing and the strip under the camera show: your next meeting, the time, your meters, your message, what's playing, or nothing. A camera light around the notch lights your face when any app turns the camera on. Off until you switch it on, no camera permission needed. Test Glow in Settings shows you the notch glow once before you turn any of it on. With the Claude Code integration, the notch can also glow when a Claude session is waiting on you. Since 2.28.0 a Mac without a notch, a Mac mini, an iMac, an external display, can draw the same wing text as a pill at the top center of the menu bar, with the same clicks, menu and glow; off by default. Since 2.30.0 the camera and mic indicators draw beside the pill's text, and since 2.31.0 Sanduhr idles at about half a percent of one core.

Now playing

The song or video playing on your Mac, from Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, Pandora or a browser tab, on a notch wing or a Desk corner with a moving position bar. Click it to play or pause. Titles never leave your Mac and are never saved.

Watchers

Small live cards on the notch or the Desk for work in flight: a CI run, a deploy, a long test, with its state, time so far and progress. Agents can put one up through the MCP server, and Claude Code's own background tasks can show up on their own. Both switches are off until you turn them on.

Your Claude Code setup, seen and carried.

Mods & Config, under Settings → Claude Code: one page per Claude Code folder that reads everything first and writes only through receipts you can undo.

What each folder really loads

Agents, skills, commands, plugins, MCP servers and settings, for every Claude Code folder on your Mac, with the file each one comes from and why one wins over another. The page reads first: nothing is run to find out what it is.

Meters above the prompt

The page opens on one row per folder with its state and a switch. Behind it, the rest of the integration: a statusline that can combine with yours, keeping the segments you pick, instead of replacing it; the notch glow when a session needs you; and an MCP server so Claude can ask about your usage. Remove takes out only what Sanduhr added and restores anything it replaced. Link an account to a folder and that folder's sessions show its meters, with the statusline naming the account.

Carry it to another Mac

Export Config reads a folder's agents, skills, commands, settings and MCP servers into one config pack: a description plus the plain files that travel, never a copy of the folder. Secrets and paths only your Mac has stay home, listed as names to set by hand in CARRY.md. Import opens the pack into any other folder as a Compare view with a checkbox per difference: only what that folder lacks comes over, and Undo Import takes it back.

Mod packs, and mods Claude proposes

A mod pack is the smallest config pack: export a kept mod, open it on any Mac, and it lands as a proposal with its files and the validator's card, to review before you keep it. Claude can propose mods and config too; nothing lands until you review it. A mod pack is also a one-plugin marketplace, so it works without Sanduhr.

Claude writes your messages and themes

With the MCP server installed, Claude Code can suggest Desk messages, with effects like a glow or a line written in by hand, and propose widget themes as a live preview. Nothing changes until you approve it.

Receipts, with exact undo

Every write Sanduhr makes to a Claude Code folder is a receipt: what it created, and the bytes it replaced. Remove and Undo put the file back byte for byte, and the backups live in Sanduhr's own folder, never in yours.

The widget, when you want it.

Shown, hidden, or on call

Always shown, hidden while Desk is on, or only when you open it. Click the meters and it appears beside them. Deep Work, the Pacing Calculators and Cooldown Snake open from the Tools menu, even with the widget hidden.

Themes you can see

A gallery of every theme, yours included, with a preview of each and one click to apply. Seven ship in the box: Obsidian, Aurora, Ember, Mint, 626 Labs, Matrix and Blueprint. Match Desk draws the widget in your Desk face and ink, so it reads like part of the desktop.

Your own themes

Drop a theme JSON into Settings → Themes, or paste what an AI agent returned. Copy Agent Prompt hands any chat agent the brief; Open Themes Folder is where your files live.

Not just a mirror of the numbers.

Sanduhr does the pacing math so you don't have to.

Burn-rate projection

"Hits 100% in ~4h 22m at current pace" warns you before you run dry, not after.

Pace markers on every bar

Colored ticks show where "on pace" is right now, so you pace by eye, not by math.

Sparkline history

Per-tier velocity trends, 30 days per account, so you can see whether you're accelerating or cooling off.

Per-model weekly meters

Each per-model weekly allowance, tracked automatically. When Anthropic adds a limit for any new model, its meter just appears, no update needed.

Claude Usage page + local vault

Local Claude Code logs become a dashboard: sessions by project with agent roll-up, weekly trends, a clickable calendar, sent/received splits, CSV export, and an opt-in vault that outlives the CLI's 30-day log cleanup.

Your usage, per account

Each account chooses what Sanduhr keeps: 30 days of meter history, a local record of its Claude Code sessions (tokens by day, model and project, never what was written), and what Claude may see, off by default. The Claude Usage page shows it as overview, trends and sessions, with CSV export.

Accounts, alerts, and one Settings window.

Sign in without DevTools

Sign in to claude.ai in a Sanduhr window and your session key is saved the moment you're in: no DevTools, no copying cookies. The window keeps nothing once it closes. Continue with Google works too: Google's sign-in opens in a small window of its own and hands back to claude.ai, with your browser and a pasted key as the way out only if Google's page stays blank.

More than one account

Add each Claude login under a name, like Work or Team, and switch from the widget's account chip, any Sanduhr menu, or the account name on Desk. Each account keeps its own history, so the sparklines never mix. Follow the account I'm using, off by default, switches for you when the signal is clear.

Smarter alerts, one Settings window

A pace warning when a limit is on course to run out before it resets, quiet hours and a sound of your choice, all off until you turn them on. Every setting sits in one window, Updates and About included, and a shortcut you pick, Option+S by default, opens it while Desk is on.

Threshold alerts

A toast and a soft procedural chime when a pool crosses your warn or urgent line. Per-model meters stay politely silent by default.

Install.

Recommended macOS 14 or newer · Apple silicon and Intel

Homebrew

One line taps and installs. Updates come with brew upgrade --cask sanduhr, or from the app's own daily check.

brew install --cask estevanhernandez-stack-ed/tap/sanduhr The tap on GitHub
macOS 14 or newer · Apple silicon and Intel

The .dmg

Download the latest Sanduhr-<version>.dmg and drag it to Applications. Signed with Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so no Gatekeeper warning. It checks for updates daily when automatic checks are on, or right away from Check for Updates… in any Sanduhr menu.

On Windows?

The Windows build is its own app from the same repo: the widget, a statusline and an MCP server for Claude Code, free on the Microsoft Store. Sanduhr für Claude on Windows.

✓ No data comes back to us.

No telemetry. No analytics. No crash reporter. No usage metrics. 626 Labs has no server and no pipeline to receive data from Sanduhr, even in principle.

The app talks to claude.ai over HTTPS, using your own session cookie, to read your own usage numbers, and checks for its own updates. When you ask it to, it also runs Claude Code's own install commands and pushes a mod to your own marketplace repo; none of that reaches 626 Labs. Your cookie stays in the macOS Keychain. Settings → Accounts → Sign Out removes it from this machine; your claude.ai session itself stays signed in. Uninstalling doesn't clear the Keychain; Settings → About → Reset Sanduhr… removes your keys and everything else Sanduhr wrote.

Claude sees your usage only if you install Sanduhr's MCP server and choose, per account, to share meters or activity. Sharing is off by default, and what Claude Code reads goes to Anthropic like the rest of your conversation, not to us.
